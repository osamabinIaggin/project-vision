What this model does and does not do

Two independent screens are shown over the same ground. The network layer is a hydraulic capacity calculation: Manning conveyance from each segment's surveyed cross-section, against the runoff its allocated catchment delivers. The water layer is a HAND threshold — the terrain lying below a given stage above the drainage network.

Neither is a hydrodynamic simulation, and the two are not coupled. No open elevation product resolves Accra's street micro-topography, so routing water across a 30 m surface over a flat coastal plain would produce a confident-looking result with no physical content. This shows where the drainage system is overwhelmed and where standing water is possible — not where any particular flow goes.

Siltation is a scenario, not an observation. Which drains are actually obstructed is the open question the project leaves; the survey records no usable proxy for it. Rainfall benchmarks come from an ERA5 relation known to understate Accra's convective intensities, so failure counts read as lower bounds.
